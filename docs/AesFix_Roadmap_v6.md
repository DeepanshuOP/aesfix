## 0. Verdict on one page

**Finding 1 — The repository is a decent scaffold wrapped around fabricated results.** Phases 1–6 (~46 commits) landed between 24 and 28 September. In those five days the docs claim a GRPO-trained Qwen2.5-VL-7B, a 300–500-pair human study, GPT-4o and Claude evaluations, "community submissions" from Meta AI and OpenGVLab, and a Fitzpatrick-stratified bias audit. None of these happened. The leaderboard numbers are literal constants in `aesfix/eval/leaderboard.py`; the "frontier VLM" is a function that returns hardcoded contrast/saturation/white-balance fixes with random jitter; the human studies are generated by a noise model; the critic is a luminance heuristic scored by a luminance-based mock judge, which is the CritiQ self-referential-SRCC bug reborn. The 198 passing tests pass because they assert against the same simulated values. Details and file-level evidence are in Part I.

**Finding 2 — The v5 novelty is no longer open.** The "empty quadrant" (critique + execute + verify) is now occupied by at least six published systems: JarvisArt (NeurIPS 2025; MLLM + 200 Lightroom tools + GRPO-R), MonetGPT (SIGGRAPH 2025; critique → procedural fix → execute), PhotoArtAgent (reflection loop that re-evaluates executed edits), PhotoAgent (Feb 2026; closed perceive–plan–execute–evaluate loop), RetouchIQ (CVPR 2026, Adobe; executable Lightroom edits + learned reward), and JarvisEvo (editor–evaluator co-optimization). In text, "judge a critique by whether its fix works" is RealCritic, and "train the critic by RL on fix success" is CTRL. On the patent side, Adobe's US 11,990,156 already covers scoring before/after applying a preset and ranking presets by the improvement, which is AesFix's product loop almost verbatim. Details in Part II.

**Finding 3 — The problem every one of those papers names, and none solves, is the verifier.** RetouchIQ states outright that the field lacks reliable, verifiable rewards; DEAR (2025) shows off-the-shelf IQA models barely beat a total-variation baseline at predicting which of two renderings humans prefer; reward-hacking studies show aesthetic and preference judges are gameable. Every agent above says "my edit improved the photo" on the word of a judge nobody has validated at the granularity of an edit. **Nobody offers a guarantee.**

**New thesis (one line):** *Every retouching agent now claims its edits are improvements. AesFix certifies it — with a finite-sample statistical guarantee against human preference — falsifies each fix at the level of the aspect it claimed to fix, and tells you when to leave the photo alone.*

### II.3 What is actually open

No searched work provides (a) a **finite-sample guarantee** that edits an agent applies are ones humans prefer, (b) **aspect-level falsification** of a fix (did it improve the aspect it claimed, without collateral damage to others?), (c) a controlled **abstention** verdict ("no certified improvement — leave it"), or (d) a **human-labelled map of where aesthetic judges and humans disagree on edit-induced changes** across op types and magnitudes. These are cheap to build, need no large training run, and turn every competitor into something AesFix *evaluates* rather than competes with. Before submission, repeat a targeted search (terms: "certified", "guarantee", "selective", "conformal", "risk control" × "retouching / photo editing / aesthetic") because the field moves monthly.

## Part III — The new thesis and contributions

**N1 — Certified Aesthetic Gain (core; paper + patent).** A certifier that takes any proposed edit, measures it with a multi-judge + aspect-probe feature vector, and certifies it only if a threshold calibrated on human pairwise preferences guarantees, with probability ≥ 1−δ, that the rate of certified edits humans would reject is ≤ α. Built on selective classification with guaranteed risk (Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017) and Learn-then-Test risk control (Angelopoulos et al.). Includes group-conditional (Mondrian) calibration by advice source and genre, and an explicit shift test: calibrate on some advice sources, certify another.

**N2 — VERIFY-Bench and the Goodhart Map (dataset + finding).** ~1,500 (original, edited) pairs from six advice sources plus a controlled op-magnitude sweep, each judged by 5 crowd raters (3-way: edited / original / no difference), with an expert subset. Used to (i) calibrate N1, (ii) score every judge's agreement with humans on edit-induced deltas, and (iii) draw the Goodhart Map: per op and magnitude, where judges say "better" and humans say "worse". Headline figure: exploitability index per judge.

**N3 — Aspect-falsifiable advice metrics (AG v2).** A consistent metric family: best-of-k and compositional gain (signed), human win rate, harm rate, targeted-aspect gain, collateral damage, and aspect verification rate. Replaces the inconsistent v5 AG@k.

**N4 — Certified reward for RL (optional).** Train a small critic (Qwen3-VL-2B/4B + LoRA, Tier-A op grid with precomputed rewards) under (a) raw-judge reward vs. (b) certified-verifier reward; measure human win rate and hack-zone frequency. Only if N1 works and budget allows.

**N5 — Verified-counterfactual coaching RCT (HCI).** Does showing a photographer *their own photo, fixed and certified*, teach more than a text critique? Three-arm randomized study over two weeks. Outcome: blinded pairwise judgments of pre vs. post photos. Nobody has run this for general photography; it directly tests the Skill Curriculum idea and needs no GPU.

**Paper A** = N1 + N2 + N3, benchmarking real advice sources (prompted Qwen3-VL, two frontier APIs, JarvisArt and MonetGPT if their released weights run, random and no-op controls, synthetic oracle). **Paper B** = N5. **Paper C** = N4 or folded into A.

## Part IV — Formal specification (one consistent set of definitions)

### IV.1 Objects

Image x. Advice set A(x) = (a₁ … a_k), each aᵢ = (op, params, aspect αᵢ ∈ {composition, lighting, color, clarity, technical}, direction dᵢ ∈ {+1, −1}, rationale). Executor E. Single edit xᵢ = E(x, aᵢ). Composed edit x₁:ₖ = E(x, a₁ ∘ … ∘ aₖ).

Judges s₁ … s_M, each z-normalised on the reference pool so deltas are comparable: δₘ(x, x′) = zₘ(x′) − zₘ(x). Ensemble delta Δ(x, x′) = trimmed mean over m; disagreement σ(x, x′) = std over m.

### IV.2 Gain metrics (never mix these)

- **Best-of-k (signed):** AG_best@k = 𝔼ₓ[ maxᵢ≤ₖ Δ(x, xᵢ) ]. Non-decreasing in k by construction — add a property test.
- **Compositional (signed):** AG_comp@k = 𝔼ₓ[ Δ(x, x₁:ₖ) ]. May decrease with k.
- Clipped variants (max(0,·)) are reported only as secondary columns and labelled.
- **Human win rate:** HWR = 𝔼[ 1{humans prefer x′} + ½·1{tie} ] on the human-labelled subset.
- **Harm rate:** HR = P(humans prefer x | the system applied x′).

### IV.3 Aspect falsification

Aspect probes p_α (SigLIP linear probes on AADB/SPAQ attributes; report held-out SRCC). Noise floor ε_β measured by re-scoring the same image under JPEG re-encode and ±2% resize jitter.

- Targeted aspect gain: TAGᵢ = dᵢ · (p_{αᵢ}(xᵢ) − p_{αᵢ}(x)).
- Collateral: COLᵢ = Σ_{β≠αᵢ} max(0, −(p_β(xᵢ) − p_β(x)) − ε_β).
- Aspect verification rate: AVR = P(TAG > ε ∧ COL = 0).
- Validate probes against humans on a 200-pair subset ("which image has better lighting?").

### IV.4 Certified Gain (N1)

Feature vector φ(x, x′) = [δ₁ … δ_M, σ, TAG, COL, over-processing flags]. Certification score c = h(φ), where h is either training-free (e.g. the 20th percentile of judge deltas) or a small model (logistic regression / shallow GBM) fit on a training split of human-labelled pairs. Label y = 1 if the rater majority prefers x′ (ties handled per protocol).

False certification rate: FCR(λ) = P(y = 0 | c ≥ λ). Coverage: Cov(λ) = P(c ≥ λ).

Calibration on a disjoint set D_cal (image-disjoint from h's training split and from test). For each λ on a grid scanned from strictest to loosest: n_λ = #{c ≥ λ}, e_λ = #{c ≥ λ, y = 0}, p_λ = BinomCDF(e_λ; n_λ, α). Fixed-sequence testing: keep rejecting "FCR(λ) > α" while p_λ ≤ δ; λ̂ is the last rejected λ. Under exchangeability of calibration and deployment pairs, P(FCR(λ̂) ≤ α) ≥ 1 − δ.

Required honesty about the guarantee: (1) it is with respect to the labelling procedure (majority of r raters from the recruited population), not "all humans"; (2) it holds under exchangeability, so report Mondrian (per-group) calibration and a leave-one-source-out shift experiment showing where it breaks; (3) implement the test yourself (~60 lines) so every step is auditable, and cross-check against MAPIE/crepes.

Reported artefacts: realised FCR vs. target α on test, the **certification frontier** (Cov vs. α), per-source coverage, and the verifier baselines (single judge > 0, ensemble mean > 0, MLLM-as-judge "is B better than A?", DEAR's MEAR pairwise model if weights are available, learned h).

Product semantics: "Certified" badge at α = 0.10, δ = 0.05 means "with 95% confidence, at most 1 in 10 certified edits is one our human panel would reject." No certified edit ⇒ the verdict "No certified improvement — leave it, or reshoot."

### IV.5 Goodhart Map (N2)

For each op o and magnitude m on a grid, apply to a stratified set of base images; measure mean δₘ per judge and HWR. Hack zone for judge j: H_j = {(o, m): 𝔼[δ_j] > 0 ∧ HWR < 0.5}. Exploitability index = fraction of the (o, m) grid in H_j, weighted by how often real advice sources propose that (o, m).

### IV.6 Interpretable head (if kept as a research claim)

Monotonicity is not guaranteed by non-negative consequent weights in a normalised TS system. Two honest options: (a) zero-order TS on a full grid partition with shared, ordered membership functions and consequents constrained non-decreasing along each input index (cumulative-softplus parameterisation) — a known sufficient condition in the monotone fuzzy-systems literature (verify the exact reference, e.g. Won, Park & Lee, *Fuzzy Sets and Systems* 2002, before citing); or (b) report monotonicity empirically and drop the word "guarantee". Either way, C3 is supporting material, not a headline.

### IV.7 Identity guard v2

Real LPIPS (AlexNet) + DINOv2 ViT-S/14 CLS cosine + InsightFace embedding when faces are present, with τ calibrated on a labelled set of acceptable vs. unacceptable edits. Note that Tier-A global ops cannot hallucinate content; for Tier-A the relevant check is over-processing (clipped-pixel fraction, out-of-gamut saturation, halo energy), which feeds φ. The identity guard becomes mandatory when Tier-B generative edits return (R7).

## Part VII — Patent strategy (read before pushing anything new)

*Not legal advice. Take this to VIT's IPR cell through the Invention Disclosure Form process before filing.*

**Facts that constrain you.**
1. India has no general grace period. Section 31 covers only narrow cases (government-notified exhibitions, papers read before a learned society, certain trials, claimed via Form 31 within 12 months). Publishing on GitHub or arXiv is not one of them.
2. Anything already in the public repo since mid-September (AG@k, the closed loop, the pooled-RGB guard, the ANFIS head, XMP export) is prior art against your own Indian application. Making the repo private now does not undo that; it only protects what you add next.
3. Once a provisional specification is filed, later publication of the matter it describes does not defeat the patent (Section 33 effect), provided the complete specification follows within 12 months.
4. The US has a one-year inventor grace period; the EPO has none.
5. Check VIT's IP policy: work using university resources may be university-owned or co-owned. The IPR cell route handles this.

**Candidate claim (N1), sketched for the disclosure form.** A computer-implemented method for automatically applying image edits comprising: receiving candidate edit operations each associated with a declared target image attribute and direction; executing each candidate on the image; computing a verification vector comprising deltas from a plurality of image-quality models, their dispersion, the change in the declared attribute and changes in non-declared attributes; mapping the vector to a certification score; comparing the score against a threshold selected by a statistical risk-control procedure on human pairwise preference data such that, with confidence 1−δ, the proportion of certified edits for which humans prefer the unedited image is at most α; applying or exporting only certified edits; and embedding a certificate identifying α, δ, and the calibration version in the edited file's provenance metadata. Dependent claims: per-group calibration keyed on edit source and scene category; an abstention output when no candidate is certified; recalibration triggered by drift in the verification-vector distribution; rejection of edits whose off-target attribute degradation exceeds a measured noise floor.

**Prior art the examiner will cite, and the honest distinction.** US 11,990,156 ranks presets by before/after score difference but gives no statistical guarantee against human preference and no attribute falsification. US 11,574,630 moves attribute scores but does not certify. JarvisArt/RetouchIQ/PhotoAgent evaluate edits with learned rewards but without guarantees. Risk: an examiner may argue that combining known risk-control methods with known before/after scoring is obvious. **Realistic odds: modest.** The paper is the more reliable deliverable; the patent is a cheap option if filed before disclosure. If the IPR cell's search finds close art, skip the patent and publish — the paper then functions as a defensive publication.

**Operational rule from today:** all N1 code, calibration results, and paper drafts live in a **private** repository until the provisional filing receipt exists. The filing date must precede the arXiv date.
